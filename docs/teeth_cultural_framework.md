# Caribbean Teeth Dream Framework

Status: Approved working cultural framework for Jamaican Dream Interpreter
Readiness: 56/100, NOT READY until live regression gates pass

## Attribution

These meanings are documented as Caribbean cultural dream tradition and Jamaican True Stories working doctrine, not as scientifically established predictions. Jamaican True Stories acts as curator/interpreter of the cultural framework.

Do not present these meanings as scientifically proven predictions or guaranteed outcomes. Product language should identify them as cultural dream-omen interpretations and preserve the distinction between warning and certainty.

## Approved meanings

### Teeth generally
Teeth dreams belong to a death-omen / family-warning cluster in Caribbean cultural tradition.

### Dreamer's own teeth
When the dream involves the dreamer's own tooth or teeth, the warning concerns a relative, close friend, or someone within the dreamer's relationship circle.

### One tooth falls out
A one-person death omen/warning concerning one person within the approved relationship scope. This is a warning, not a guaranteed death outcome.

### Multiple teeth fall out
A multiple-person death warning. Multiple lost teeth correspond to warnings concerning multiple people, preserving the one-tooth/one-person distinction.

### Pain proximity and intensity modifier
Pain accompanying tooth loss has two approved functions:

1. Painful tooth loss points toward a close relative or person very close to the dreamer.
2. Pain increases the emotional intensity of the underlying warning.

Painless tooth loss points toward a friend, acquaintance, or more distant person.

Do not use pain to change the one-tooth/one-person or multiple-teeth/multiple-person count. Do not confuse physical tooth pain with the dreamer's emotional reaction to the dream.

### Loose or wobbly tooth
A sickness warning concerning someone. This is not a medical diagnosis and must not guarantee illness.

### Bleeding gums
Standalone bleeding gums are a warning that a bad omen may be approaching. Treat bleeding gums as a warning/escalation state, not as a separate invented death count and not as proof of a blood-relative relationship.

Where the dream explicitly attributes gum bleeding to brushing, flossing, an injury, dental work, or another physical cause, preserve that causal context rather than forcing the standalone omen rule.

### Blood on a fallen tooth
Blood on a fallen tooth increases the severity or intensity of the underlying tooth-loss warning.

Blood does not independently determine the person, relationship, quantity, actor, or outcome. Do not convert blood into a blood-relative rule.

### Cancellation and restorative endings
A Teeth warning is not to be narrated as inevitable. The framework includes the possibility that an omen can be cancelled, but exact cancellation mechanisms/rules must come from approved source material before they are encoded.

A later restorative event, such as a loose tooth becoming firm again, should be preserved as an actual later ending. Until a specific cancellation meaning is approved, the interpreter must not invent one.

## Structural distinctions awaiting confirmation or source reconciliation

The interpreter must detect these independently even when their final cultural meaning is not yet approved:

- front tooth / front teeth
- back tooth / back teeth / molars
- upper teeth
- lower teeth
- dreamer ownership versus another person's teeth beyond the approved dreamer-own-teeth rule
- broken or cracked teeth
- combinations of falling, looseness, bleeding, breakage, position, pain, ownership, blood, and restoration

Front/back and upper/lower may be captured as structural modifiers but must not be assigned an unverified relationship class. Do not import cross-cultural positional mappings into the Caribbean doctrine layer without approved Caribbean evidence.

Do not fabricate meanings for pending distinctions. Mark unresolved mappings as needing doctrine confirmation.

## Backend factuality rules

The Teeth cluster must distinguish actual dream events from:

- negated events: "my tooth did not fall out"
- hypothetical/future events: "my tooth might fall out"
- near-miss events: "my tooth almost fell out"
- negated states: "my tooth was not broken", "my gums were not bleeding"
- physical tooth pain versus emotional hurt
- standalone bleeding gums versus blood on a fallen tooth
- bleeding with an explicit physical cause
- a loose tooth that remains loose versus a later restorative event
- generic body falling that occurs separately from a tooth falling out

## Interpretation-language guardrail

Preferred framing:

"In Caribbean dream tradition, this is treated as a warning..."

or

"This dream is read in Jamaican True Stories doctrine as a warning..."

Avoid claiming:

"This dream proves someone will die."

"This guarantees someone is sick."

The cultural framework should be preserved accurately without converting tradition into documented fact.
