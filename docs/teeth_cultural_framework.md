# Caribbean Teeth Dream Framework

Status: Approved working cultural framework for Jamaican Dream Interpreter

## Attribution

These meanings are documented as Caribbean cultural dream tradition, not as doctrine originated by Jamaican True Stories. Jamaican True Stories acts as curator/interpreter of the cultural framework.

Do not present these meanings as scientifically proven predictions or guaranteed outcomes. Product language should identify them as cultural dream-omen interpretations and preserve the distinction between warning and certainty.

## Approved meanings

### Teeth generally
Teeth dreams belong to a death-omen / family-warning cluster in Caribbean cultural tradition.

### One tooth falls out
A warning associated with the possible death of one person close to the dreamer or a family member, unless the omen is cancelled.

### Multiple teeth fall out
A multiple-person death warning. Multiple lost teeth can correspond to warnings concerning multiple people.

### Loose or wobbly tooth
A sickness warning concerning someone.

### Bleeding gums
A warning around the omen and a prompt to begin praying. Treat this as an escalation/warning modifier, not as a separate invented death count.

### Cancellation
A teeth death omen is not to be narrated as inevitable. The framework includes the possibility that an omen can be cancelled. Exact cancellation mechanisms/rules must come from approved source material before they are encoded.

Do not fabricate meanings for these pending distinctions. Mark unresolved mappings as needing doctrine confirmation.

## Backend factuality rules already established

The Teeth cluster must distinguish actual dream events from:

- negated events: "my tooth did not fall out"
- hypothetical/future events: "my tooth might fall out"
- near-miss events: "my tooth almost fell out"
- negated states: "my tooth was not broken", "my gums were not bleeding"
- generic body falling that occurs separately from a tooth falling out

## Interpretation-language guardrail

Preferred framing:

"In Caribbean dream tradition, this is treated as a warning..."

Avoid claiming:

"This dream proves someone will die."

The cultural framework should be preserved accurately without converting tradition into documented fact.
